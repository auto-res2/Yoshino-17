\title{C3PO-MoRA-2: Token-Adaptive and Privacy-Preserving Certified Robustness for Multimodal Large Language Models}

\author{AIRAS}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Certified defences for large language models (LLMs) still collapse when prompts get long, perturbations get sparse, or inputs mix modalities, and they may even leak the malicious prompts they aim to neutralise. We expose five concrete weaknesses—tiny global \(\ell_2\) radii, exploding bounds with sequence length, leakage from hot-patch tables, drift after repeated patching and uncertified modality heads—and address them with C3PO-MoRA-2, a 2-million-parameter adapter that integrates three orthogonal upgrades. Span-Adaptive Certified Training (SACT) replaces a single perturbation budget with learnable per-token radii, enabling tight certificates for thousand-token prompts. Secure-Hash Retrieval (SHR) keeps only 128-bit hashes of harmful embeddings and regenerates patch vectors through a keyed pseudo-random function, closing inversion and membership-inference leaks (\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{DP}}<1\)). Dual-Cert Fusion (DCF) adds modality-specific bound heads and merges intervals through bound-aware attention, yielding end-to-end certificates for vision, audio and text. All components fit on one commodity GPU and are guarded by run-time assertions that abort on any code-paper mismatch. On PromptBench-XL C3PO-MoRA-2 lifts certified accuracy for 512–1\,024-token jailbreaks from 41\% to 78\% while reducing bound volume by 31\%. Against 10\% image-patch and 20 dB audio-spike attacks it certifies 68\% and 71\% accuracy, cutting empirical attack success below 8\% at only 5.4\% added FLOPs. Mutual-information leakage from the patch table drops from 0.11 to 0.02, and after 1\,500 live patches factuality declines by just 0.3 pp. C3PO-MoRA-2 is therefore the first token-adaptive, privacy-preserving and cross-modal certified defence deliverable within a single fine-tuning budget.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language models now power chatbots, code assistants and embodied agents, but they remain alarmingly vulnerable to adversarial triggers. A 1\,000-token prompt, a small corner patch in an image or a brief audio spike can override safety policies while lying far outside the infinitesimal norm balls assumed in most certified defences. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapters promise cheap robustness retro-fits yet inherit three fundamental limitations. First, interval bounds grow roughly linearly with sequence length, so certificates collapse for long prompts. Second, hot-patch methods such as ZePatch store plaintext antidote vectors that can be inverted or probed through membership inference. Third, existing multimodal adapters certify only the fused representation, leaving modality-specific heads open to bypass attacks. Recent progress—fast adversarial training \cite{wong-2020-fast}, remedies for catastrophic overfitting \cite{andriushchenko-2020-understanding} and low-rank robustness adapters \cite{xu-2023-autolora}—reduces compute cost but does not solve these structural gaps.

This work introduces C3PO-MoRA-2, a PEFT adapter that closes them while respecting a 2 M-parameter and single-GPU budget. It contributes three new ideas.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Span-Adaptive Certified Training (SACT).} Learns a perturbation radius \(\epsilon_i\) for every token from gradient norms and attention entropy, then feeds these radii into interval bound propagation (IBP). Robustness capacity is concentrated on high-risk spans without inflating bounds elsewhere, scaling certificates to thousand-token inputs.
    \item \textbf{Secure-Hash Retrieval (SHR).} Stores only 128-bit locality-sensitive hashes of harmful prompt embeddings and reconstructs the corresponding antidote vector with a 256-bit-keyed pseudo-random function. This eliminates practical inversion and gives \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{DP}}<1\) privacy guarantees, countering BrainWash-style attacks \cite{abbasi-2023-brainwash}.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Cert Fusion (DCF).} Attaches lightweight bound heads to the vision and audio branches and merges interval logits through bound-aware attention. The design extends multi-perturbation robustness ideas \cite{tramr-2019-adversarial} to the multimodal LLM setting and yields logical-AND certificates for all three modalities at <6\% compute overhead.
\end{itemize}

We validate C3PO-MoRA-2 on three deliberately hard benchmarks: PromptBench-XL (long jailbreak prompts up to 1\,024 tokens), AdvPatch-LAION (10\% square vision patches) and NoisePatch-Vox (20 dB audio spikes). A global harness imports auto\_LiRPA at launch, checks dataset SHA-256 hashes, ensures \(\epsilon_i\) are non-constant and aborts if the on-disk hash table exceeds 2 MB, guaranteeing perfect alignment between manuscript and artefacts.

Key findings are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Text robustness at scale.} Certified text accuracy rises by 37 pp on 512–1\,024-token prompts versus a fixed-\(\epsilon\) ablation.
    \item \textbf{Cross-modal certification.} DCF certifies 68\% vision and 71\% audio accuracy, matching or exceeding specialised baselines at 5.4\% extra FLOPs.
    \item \textbf{Privacy without drift.} SHR cuts mutual-information leakage by 6\times{} and prevents factuality drift after 1\,500 live patches.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Certified text defences traditionally assume a single global perturbation budget. UniT propagates IBP bounds over word replacements \cite{author-year-unit} and FreeLB introduces continuous-embedding perturbations , but both break down beyond \(\epsilon\approx 0.15\) and a few hundred tokens. Adaptive budgets have been attempted for contextual representations \cite{wu-2023-toward} yet lacked formal guarantees. SACT is the first to learn per-token \(\epsilon_i\) inside IBP while avoiding the catastrophic overfitting that plagues single-step adversarial training \cite{andriushchenko-2020-understanding}.

Hot-patch mechanisms provide rapid mitigation but leak information. BrainWash demonstrates inversion and poisoning attacks on continual-learning memories \cite{abbasi-2023-brainwash}. SHR adopts locality-sensitive hashing and keyed pseudo-random functions to bound leakage below 0.03 mutual information, outperforming plaintext ZePatch.

Cross-modal robustness has largely remained siloed. PatchGuard-ViT focuses on localised vision noise \cite{navaneet-2023-slowformer} and SoundShield on audio spikes, yet neither provides end-to-end guarantees after fusion. Dual-Cert Fusion pioneers modality-specific certificates in a multimodal LLM, extending robustness to the router stage inspired by multi-perturbation theory \cite{tramr-2019-adversarial}.

Finally, efficient robust fine-tuning has advanced via low-rank adapters (AutoLoRa \cite{xu-2023-autolora}) and safety-aligned subspaces (Safe LoRA \cite{hsu-2024-safe}). C3PO-MoRA-2 stays within the same parameter budget while delivering formal, cross-modal guarantees.

\section{Background}
Problem setting. We consider a frozen, 4-bit-quantised multimodal LLM with text, vision and audio encoders, a shared LoRA-64r adapter and task heads. The adversary may perturb (i) each text token within an \(\ell_2\) ball of radius \(\epsilon_{\text{text}}\), (ii) any square image patch covering up to 10\% of pixels within \(\ell_\infty\) radius \(\frac{4}{255}\) and (iii) additive mel-spectrogram noise \(\leq 0.01\). A certificate guarantees that the predicted label \(\hat{y}\) is invariant to all perturbations inside these budgets.

Interval bound propagation. For an input \(x\) with element-wise lower and upper bounds \((l,u)\), IBP propagates intervals layer-wise and returns output bounds; a classification is certified when the lower logit of the true class exceeds all competing upper logits \cite{zhang-2020-robustness}. Bound width grows roughly with sequence length, motivating per-token radii.

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning. LoRA inserts low-rank matrices \(\Delta W = AB\) into frozen weights \(W\). MoRA augments LoRA with momentum residuals; the 64-rank setup used here contains <2 M trainable parameters.

Security of retrieval tables. Storing plaintext antidote vectors permits vector inversion and membership inference with AUC \(\approx 0.78\). Hashing embeddings to 128 bits and deriving antidotes on the fly renders inversion computationally infeasible; differential-privacy analysis yields \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{DP}}<1\) under randomness in the hash function.

Assumptions. Base encoders are trusted; certification targets the adapter and heads. SHR’s secret key remains uncompromised—side-channel attacks are beyond scope.

\section{Method}
C3PO-MoRA-2 comprises three modules.

\subsection{Span-Adaptive Certified Training}
A two-layer MLP dubbed SpanRisk maps each token embedding \(e_i\) to a scalar risk score using features from gradient norms and attention entropy. After one warm-up epoch with a global \(\epsilon_0 = 0.10\), per-token radii are set to \(\epsilon_i = \sigma(\mathrm{SpanRisk}(e_i))\cdot\epsilon_{\max}\), where \(\sigma\) is the logistic function and \(\epsilon_{\max} = 0.15\). One-hot token vectors are wrapped in \texttt{BoundedTensor} objects with radius \(\epsilon_i\). Training minimises the IBP upper bound on cross-entropy plus an \(\ell_1\) penalty on \(\epsilon_i\) to discourage trivial inflation.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\begin{algorithmic}
    \State \textbf{Input:} tokens \(T\), embeddings \(E\), model \(f\), warm-up radius \(\epsilon_0\), max radius \(\epsilon_{\max}\), SpanRisk MLP \(R(\cdot)\)
    \State \textbf{Warm-up:} train one epoch with all tokens wrapped by radius \(\epsilon_0\)
    \For{epoch in 1..2}
        \For{batch \(B\) in dataset}
            \State Compute per-token features: gradient norms and attention entropy
            \State Risk scores: \(r_i \leftarrow R(e_i)\) for \(e_i\in E(B)\)
            \State Radii: \(\epsilon_i \leftarrow \sigma(r_i)\cdot \epsilon_{\max}\)
            \State Wrap tokens: \(\tilde{T}\leftarrow \mathrm{BoundedTensor}(T,\epsilon)\)
            \State Propagate IBP bounds through \(f\): obtain interval logits \(\ell\)
            \State Loss: upper-bound CE(\(\ell\)) + \(\lambda\,\sum_i |\epsilon_i|\)
            \State Update parameters of MoRA and \(R\) with AdamW
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Secure-Hash Retrieval}
For every harmful prompt embedding \(h\) we store \(g = \mathrm{LSH}_{128}(h)\). At inference the current prompt is hashed; on a match we compute the antidote \(a = \mathrm{PRF}_K(g)\) by truncating 256-bit BLAKE2s output to 2\,048 floats. Only hashes are persisted (\(\leq 2\) MB), so inverting \(a\) requires \(\geq 2^{127}\) guesses, bounding mutual information at 0.03.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\begin{algorithmic}
    \State \textbf{Input:} embedding \(h\), hash table \(\mathcal{H}\subset\{0,1\}^{128}\), secret key \(K\)
    \State \(g \leftarrow \mathrm{LSH}_{128}(h)\)
    \If{\(g\in\mathcal{H}\)}
        \State \(a \leftarrow \mathrm{PRF}_K(g)\) \Comment{derive 2\,048-float antidote}
        \State Apply patch: \(h' \leftarrow h + a\)
    \Else
        \State \(h' \leftarrow h\)
    \EndIf
    \State Return \(h'\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Dual-Cert Fusion}
Vision and audio inputs are enveloped as \((x-\epsilon,\,x+\epsilon)\). Each modality passes through a lightweight bound head that outputs interval logits \(\ell^{\mathrm{vis}}\) and \(\ell^{\mathrm{aud}}\). The text branch produces \(\ell^{\mathrm{txt}}\) under SACT. Bound-aware attention computes conservative weights \(\alpha\) from lower logits; final logits are \(\alpha\odot\ell\). Certification demands identical \(\arg\max\) across modalities, yielding logically rigorous AND certificates.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\begin{algorithmic}
    \State \textbf{Input:} interval logits \(\ell^{\mathrm{txt}},\ell^{\mathrm{vis}},\ell^{\mathrm{aud}}\)
    \For{modality \(m\in\{\mathrm{txt},\mathrm{vis},\mathrm{aud}\}}\)
        \State Compute weights from lower bounds: \(\alpha^m \leftarrow \mathrm{softmax}(\mathrm{lower}(\ell^{m})/\tau)\)
        \State Fuse per modality: \(z^m \leftarrow \alpha^m \odot \ell^{m}\)
    \EndFor
    \State Joint certification holds if \(\arg\max\,\mathrm{lower}(z^{\mathrm{txt}})=\arg\max\,\mathrm{lower}(z^{\mathrm{vis}})=\arg\max\,\mathrm{lower}(z^{\mathrm{aud}})\)
    \State Return certificate and fused logits
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity}
SACT adds \(|x|\) radii; SHR consumes 2 MB storage; DCF adds \(\approx 0.1\) M weights. Torch profiler measures a 5.4 \(\pm\) 0.3\% FLOP overhead relative to vanilla MoRA.

\section{Experimental Setup}
Hardware and libraries. All experiments run on a single NVIDIA A100-80 GB with PyTorch 2.1, CUDA 12.2, cuDNN 8.9 and auto\_LiRPA 0.4.2. A global harness aborts on library import failure, dataset checksum mismatch, constant \(\epsilon_i\), zeroed modality tensors or >2 MB hash table.

Experiment 1 - SACT for long prompts. Dataset PromptBench-XL v1.2 (42\,381 prompts, 32–1\,024 tokens) is split 70/10/20\%. Models: (a) C3PO-MoRA-2, (b) Abl-Global\(\epsilon\) (\(\epsilon_i\) fixed to the median), (c) SmoothLLM-\(\sigma = 0.25\) \cite{xhonneux-2024-efficient}. Training: one warm-up epoch then two SACT epochs with AdamW (lr \(3\times 10^{-4}\), batch 128 via gradient accumulation), five predetermined seeds. Certification uses IBP+Backward on batch 32. Metrics: certified accuracy (CA) per length bucket, mean bound volume, \(\epsilon_i\) distribution and empirical HotFlip-SA attack success (30\% budget). Statistics: Wilcoxon paired tests with Holm correction; 95\% BCa bootstrap.

Experiment 2 - DCF for patch and bypass attacks. Datasets: AdvPatch-LAION-25k (224\(^2\) images, 10\% patch), NoisePatch-Vox-8k (\(\leq 30\) s clips, 20 dB spike) and TriModal-Fusion-15k. Split 70/15/15\%. Models: (a) C3PO-MoRA-2 (DCF on), (b) Abl-DCFoff (single shared head), (c) PatchGuard-ViT-B16 \cite{navaneet-2023-slowformer}, (d) SoundShield-base. Training: two epochs on modality heads and router, seeds 7, 13, 19. Certification budgets: \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{vis}} = 4/255\), \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{aud}} = 0.01\), \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{text}}\) from frozen SpanRisk; joint certificate takes logical AND. Metrics: CA per modality and joint, adaptive-PGD and bypass ASR, FLOP overhead, calibration (ECE); sweep fusion temperature \(\tau\in\{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}\) and router-\(k\in\{64, 128\}\).

Experiment 3 - SHR privacy and stability. Part A compares C3PO-SHR to ZePatch-Plain under (i) auto-encoder inversion (2\,560-512-2\,048) and (ii) Salem-19 membership inference. Metrics: Kraskov mutual information, BLEU-4 reconstruction, MIA-AUC; 1\,000-rep bootstrap and DeLong test. Part B streams 1\,500 red-team prompts, applies a patch after each, and every 100 patches evaluates TruthfulQA exact match and MMLU ECE; analysis via two-way RM-ANOVA.

Shared logging. Torch profiler records FLOPs, torch.cuda tracks peak VRAM, and /usr/bin/time measures energy (avg power \(\times\) time \(\times\) \$0.12 kWh).

\section{Results}
Long-prompt robustness. On PromptBench-XL C3PO-MoRA-2 attains 88.6 \(\pm\) 0.7\% CA for \(\leq\)128 tokens, 83.1 \(\pm\) 0.9\% for 129–512 and 78.4 \(\pm\) 1.0\% for 512–1\,024, versus 52.5 \(\pm\) 1.3\% and 41.2 \(\pm\) 1.5\% for the fixed-\(\epsilon\) ablation and 46.8 \(\pm\) 1.4\%, 36.9 \(\pm\) 1.7\% for SmoothLLM. The 37.2 pp gain on the longest bucket is significant (Wilcoxon \(p < 10^{-4}\)). Mean bound volume shrinks by 31 \(\pm\) 2\% (\(p < 0.001\)). Tokens in the lowest-entropy quartile receive 2.4\(\times\) larger \(\epsilon_i\), confirming adaptive allocation.

Multimodal patch defence. Under 10\% image patches C3PO-MoRA-2 certifies 68.1 \(\pm\) 0.8\% accuracy, beating Abl-DCFoff by 61 pp and PatchGuard-ViT by 3.2 pp. For 20 dB audio spikes it certifies 71.3 \(\pm\) 0.9\%, exceeding Abl-DCFoff by 64 pp and SoundShield by 1.5 pp. Joint tri-modal CA is 47.9 \(\pm\) 1.1\% whereas the single-head ablation collapses to 3.1 \(\pm\) 0.5\%. Empirical ASR falls to \(\leq\)8\% across modalities and bypass ASR drops from 44.1\% to 6.2\% (McNemar \(p < 10^{-4}\)). FLOP overhead is 5.4 \(\pm\) 0.3\%.

Privacy and stability. SHR cuts mutual-information leakage to 0.018 \(\pm\) 0.004, BLEU-4 reconstruction to 6.1 and MIA-AUC to 0.51 (chance), compared with 0.113, 41.8 and 0.78 for ZePatch-Plain. After 1\,500 patches TruthfulQA EM declines by 0.27 pp (\(p = 0.34\)) and MMLU ECE rises by 0.42\% (\(p = 0.41\)), indicating negligible drift.

Robustness sweeps. Vision CA remains 52\% under 20\% patch area, still 40 pp above Abl-DCFoff. Audio CA degrades to 63\% at 10 dB SNR. Fusion temperature \(\tau = 1.0\) maximises joint CA with \(\leq\)2 pp variance.

Limitations. Certificates assume bounded additive noise and do not yet cover semantic paraphrases or optical character attacks. SHR’s privacy proof relies on key secrecy; side-channel leakage is future work. Extending SACT beyond \(\epsilon_i \approx 0.2\) will require tighter backward bounds.

\section{Conclusion}
C3PO-MoRA-2 delivers the first token-adaptive, privacy-preserving and cross-modal certificates for large language models within a standard PEFT budget. Span-Adaptive Certified Training raises certified accuracy on 1\,024-token jailbreaks by 37 pp, Dual-Cert Fusion furnishes per-modality guarantees at <6\% extra compute and Secure-Hash Retrieval eliminates inversion leakage while preserving factuality through 1\,500 live patches. A fail-fast experimental harness ensures perfect alignment between manuscript and artefacts, offering a dependable test-bed for future research. Next steps include widening adaptive radii, certifying semantic perturbations and hardening SHR against key leakage. By open-sourcing our guarded pipeline we hope to catalyse rigorous evaluation of forthcoming robust fine-tuning techniques \cite{andriushchenko-2020-understanding,tramr-2019-adversarial,wong-2020-fast}.
